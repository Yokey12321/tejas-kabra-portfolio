## The build

This was a Drone Challenge UAV project for Gulf Coast Texas. The mission was to move bean bags and rings to scoring locations, one payload at a time, and land the drone.

I worked on the airframe integration, wiring, programming, and build documentation. We used a carbon-fiber quadcopter base, an STM32F411 flight controller running Betaflight, a camera, a battery pack, and reused radio and Arduino hardware.

## Making everything fit

The practical challenge was fitting the flight controller, power distribution, camera, radio, and Arduino into a small frame while keeping the wiring accessible. We planned the wire routes, tested the connections, and secured the harness after checking the power and control paths.

Tight spacing made some solder joints difficult. The prototype worked, but the wiring and connections are among the first things I would redo now.

## Control and camera integration

I configured the motors, gyro, and PID settings in Betaflight. The Arduino bridged receiver signals to the flight controller. The camera work included checking the camera on a computer before integrating it, with I2C code using Arduino's Wire library included in our programming notes.

## CAD and budget

We sketched the frame and modeled the top and bottom plates, mounting holes, and electronics placement. The CAD work helped us check where the controller, battery, camera, wiring, and payload mechanism would fit before assembly.

Purchased materials totaled approximately **$180**: $125 for the frame, $35 for the camera, $11 for wiring, and $9 for batteries. Reusing the Arduino, controller and radio hardware, and gripper kept costs down, but meant adapting the build around parts we already had.

## What I would change

The prototype flew, and it gave me experience connecting mechanical design, electrical wiring, and flight-control software. I built it before I had PCB design experience, which makes it a useful reference point for how I would approach the same problem now.

I would start with a more rigid frame, better mounting points, and a harness designed around proper connectors. A custom flight-controller board could bring the gyro, power distribution, and interfaces into a layout that fits the airframe. For the payload, a dedicated hook or latch could make pickup less dependent on precise piloting.
