## Flight changes when the payload does

Carrying an object is not simply a matter of adding enough motor thrust. Every payload changes the aircraft's mass, center of gravity, inertia, power demand, and response to control inputs. The platform has to remain predictable from pickup through transport and release.

This project treats the drone, carrying mechanism, payload, and control system as one coupled machine rather than separate subsystems.

## System architecture

The airframe and propulsion system establish the available thrust margin. The flight controller stabilizes the vehicle while a dedicated payload mechanism handles retention and release. Power, sensing, and mechanical geometry all have to be designed around the worst-case payload rather than the unloaded drone.

> The real design target is controlled payload delivery, not merely proving that the drone can lift an object.

### Core engineering decisions

- Payload mass and center-of-gravity envelope
- Motor, propeller, and battery thrust margin
- Attachment geometry and release mechanism
- Vibration isolation for control electronics
- Loss-of-signal and low-voltage behavior
- Ground-test procedure before loaded flight

## Validation plan

Testing should increase risk gradually: mechanism cycling on the bench, restrained propulsion tests, unloaded hover, fixed-mass payload hover, controlled translation, and finally pickup and release trials.

## What I want to prove

The final result should document a safe payload limit, measurable stability under load, repeatable release behavior, and the design changes revealed by flight data. Those results will turn the platform from a visual demonstration into a defensible controls and robotics project.
