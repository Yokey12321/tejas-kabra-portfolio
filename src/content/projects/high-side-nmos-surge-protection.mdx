## Protecting a high-current 12 V load

This circuit was designed to protect and regulate power for a **12 V, 5 A load** during a **50 V input transient lasting 50 ms**. The challenge was to hold the output within its intended operating range while ensuring the pass devices and control circuitry survived the short-duration surge.

The design uses back-to-back N-channel MOSFETs on the high side, combining bidirectional blocking behavior with the lower conduction loss available from N-channel devices.

## Circuit topology

An op-amp feedback loop controls the high-side MOSFETs during regulation. Because an N-channel device requires gate voltage above its source, an LT1054 charge pump provides the elevated gate-drive rail.

The supporting network includes a zener-protected control rail, LDO regulation, and a current-sense shunt. Together, these blocks constrain the gate drive, establish the protected output, and expose load current for analysis.

> Clamping the output was only one requirement. The MOSFETs also had to remain within acceptable voltage, current, gate-drive, and pulse-dissipation limits during the surge.

## LTspice validation

I simulated the complete transient response in LTspice. The analysis focused on MOSFET linear-region operation, gate-to-source voltage limits, output regulation, and short-duration surge power dissipation.

## Engineering outcome

The simulation demonstrated how each control block behaved during startup, normal operation, surge clamping, and recovery. The most valuable portfolio evidence will be the complete schematic, component-selection calculations, transient plots, and safe-operating-area comparison for the selected MOSFET.
