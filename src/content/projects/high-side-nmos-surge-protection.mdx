## The design target

The input starts at 12 V, then jumps to **50 V for 50 ms**. The design target is a protected **12 V, 5 A load**. I designed a high-side linear protection stage, simulated its response in LTspice, and laid out the board in Altium.

The model holds the output near **13.8 V** during the pulse before returning to 12 V. This is a simulation result. The work shown here does not establish bench-tested surge survival or thermal margin.

## Topology and the thermal lesson

The high-side path uses two back-to-back **IPB120N10S403ATMA1 N-channel MOSFETs**. An **OPA197** feedback loop controls the gate drive to regulate the output. I chose an N-channel approach for the current path and built a separate supply to drive the gates above the source voltage.

My original stress estimate assumed the two series MOSFETs would share the voltage drop equally. At 50 V in, about 14 V out, and 5 A, that estimate assigned roughly **18 V and 90 W to each device**.

That assumption was the flaw in the analysis: placing two devices in series does not guarantee equal dissipation. One device can take most of the roughly **180 W total instantaneous pass-stage dissipation**. The next revision needs to check each device's operating point and transient safe operating area, then validate the thermal behavior.

> A plausible output waveform does not prove the pass devices will survive the event.

## Gate drive and feedback

The MOSFET gates need a voltage above the high-side source node. A zener-limited 12 V rail supplies an **LT1054 charge pump**, which supports gate-drive commands in the roughly **18-20 V** range. The low-voltage electronics use an **LM1117-3.3** regulator.

The feedback network compares the divided output against 3.3 V. With a **32 kΩ / 10 kΩ** divider:

<div className="data-grid">
  <div>
    <small>Divider ratio</small>
    <b>10 / (32 + 10) = 0.238</b>
  </div>
  <div>
    <small>Reference</small>
    <b>3.3 V</b>
  </div>
  <div>
    <small>Calculated clamp</small>
    <b>3.3 / 0.238 ≈ 13.9 V</b>
  </div>
</div>

That is consistent with the approximately 13.8 V simulated output. It is a clamp above the nominal supply, so load tolerance still needs to be checked against that voltage.

The 680 Ω resistor feeding the 12 V zener rail sees about 38 V during the surge, or roughly **2.1 W instantaneous dissipation**. Its pulse rating and derating need to support the event. Similarly, the LDO estimate of roughly **0.435 W at 50 mA** needs a thermal check for the actual package and board.

## LTspice result

The model simplifies some IC behavior to focus on the pass devices, gate-control loop, feedback divider, and input transient. The input pulse runs from 10 ms to 60 ms; the output remains near the calculated clamp point.

## PCB layout and next steps

The layout groups the lower-power feedback and gate-drive circuitry separately from the MOSFET current path. The board also includes MCU and CAN support circuitry.

<ProjectImage
  image={pcbLayout}
  alt="Altium layout of the transient suppression board"

/>

The next revision would focus on the stressed MOSFET: more copper around the pads, a better thermal path, and a review of trace width and heatsinking. I would verify the gate-drive headroom and individual device stress in simulation, then test the pulse response and temperature rise on the bench.
