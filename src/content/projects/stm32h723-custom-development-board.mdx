## Why build another development board?

Commercial development kits optimize for broad compatibility. This board is intended to optimize for **repeatable subsystem development**: a known power tree, accessible communications, deliberate debug access, and connectors chosen around the projects it will actually support.

The STM32H723 brings enough compute and peripheral bandwidth for controls, communications, data acquisition, and future real-time work without turning every prototype into a wiring exercise.

## Schematic strategy

The board is organized around functional blocks rather than sheet-filling convenience: power entry and regulation, MCU core requirements, clocking, debug, communications, and external I/O. Every block should be independently inspectable during bring-up.

> A development board earns its keep when a failure is easy to isolate. Test access and observability are part of the circuit, not post-layout luxuries.

### Layout decisions worth showing

- Decoupling placement and return paths
- Crystal placement and clock routing
- Layer stack and uninterrupted reference planes
- High-current versus sensitive signal regions
- Connector placement driven by bench use
- SWD and UART access during enclosure integration

## Bring-up sequence

The bring-up plan starts with resistance checks and current-limited power. Only after the rails and reset state are verified does firmware enter the loop.
