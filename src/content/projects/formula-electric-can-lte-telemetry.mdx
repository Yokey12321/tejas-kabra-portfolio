## The problem

Texas A&M Formula Electric needed to see racecar diagnostics during a run. The data was already on the CAN bus; the missing piece was a reliable way to move it off the vehicle and into the team's Grafana driver station.

I designed the telemetry PCB across two revisions, wrote the embedded firmware, and worked on bench validation and vehicle integration. The board combines an STM32G4, CAN and UART interfaces, vehicle-power regulation, and a Quectel EG25 LTE modem.

## The data path

The STM32G4 receives CAN frames, extracts the vehicle data, builds 8-byte telemetry payloads, and passes them to the EG25 over UART. The modem sends the data over LTE to the driver station.

I developed the firmware in STM32CubeIDE using STM32 HAL. The system reached approximately **3,200 packets per second**, with **20-30 ms measured end-to-end latency** across the CAN-to-LTE-to-Grafana path.

> The telemetry hardware had to observe the racecar's CAN network without disrupting it.

## What changed in R2

R1 established the basic layout, connector positions, modem interface, and CAN path. It also exposed weaknesses in the power section. The input had no fuse, and the buck stage needed a stronger design and better filtering.

R2 moved to a **four-layer PCB**, added an **eFuse**, and revised the buck stage and local filtering. The stackup provided reference planes for the CAN and UART routes, while the layout separated the power and communication sections more deliberately.

## Bring-up and testing

I used oscilloscopes and digital multimeters to check power rails and signal behavior, then brought up the communication blocks and full CAN-to-LTE path. Vehicle integration added the constraints that mattered beyond the bench: the power environment, harness connections, and the cellular network at the track.

Testing at Michigan exposed reliability problems with LTE in a crowded competition environment. Carrier priority was a possible explanation for the behavior we saw with the embedded-device SIM, but the cause was not isolated. That distinction matters when deciding what to change next.

## The next revision

I would compare the current modem and SIM against a different cellular plan and a dedicated radio link under the same track conditions. The useful comparison is delivery reliability and latency across the whole path, alongside power draw, packaging, and competition constraints.

I also want to capture synchronized vehicle-side and Grafana logs, along with scope captures and assembled-board photos. Those would make it easier to distinguish a firmware or hardware issue from a network issue.
