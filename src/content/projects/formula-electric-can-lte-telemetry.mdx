## The engineering problem

A race car produces more data than a driver can interpret in the moment. The useful system is not simply a CAN logger—it is a reliable path from **vehicle signals to remote engineering decisions** under vibration, RF uncertainty, power transients, and limited track time.

The telemetry architecture therefore has two jobs: preserve the meaning of the CAN traffic and fail predictably when the cellular link does not cooperate.

## System architecture

The design separates acquisition from transport. A CAN-facing embedded node filters and timestamps the required frames, packages them for transmission, and hands them to the LTE path. That boundary matters: a dropped cellular connection should not destabilize the vehicle bus.

> The central design question was not “How do we send CAN over LTE?” It was “How do we keep the vehicle-side behavior deterministic when LTE becomes unreliable?”

### Decisions to document

- CAN message filtering and signal selection
- Timestamp ownership and clock behavior
- Buffer depth during loss of service
- Packet format and bandwidth budget
- Reconnect strategy and stale-data handling
- Vehicle power entry and transient protection

## Validation plan

The portfolio version of this project will show evidence at three levels: a bench replay of representative CAN traffic, a network impairment test, and a track test with recorded link behavior.

## What the final case study will prove

The finished story should make the reliability argument visible: what entered the gateway, what reached the server, what happened during a connection loss, and how quickly the system recovered. The strongest result will be a synchronized plot comparing vehicle-side and remote timestamps.
