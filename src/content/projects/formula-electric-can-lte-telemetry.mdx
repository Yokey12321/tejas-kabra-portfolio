## System overview

Texas A&M Formula Electric's racecar distributes critical operating data over CAN. The telemetry system moves those diagnostics off the vehicle in real time so engineers can monitor behavior from a Grafana-based driver station rather than waiting for a post-run download.

I designed the telemetry PCB across two revisions, developed its embedded firmware, and validated the integrated system before vehicle deployment. The board combines an STM32G4 microcontroller, CAN and UART interfaces, a backplane vehicle-power input, and a SIM-enabled Quectel EG25 LTE modem.

## Hardware architecture

The board has to bridge two very different environments: a deterministic vehicle network and a cellular modem with demanding power and communication requirements. CAN traffic enters the STM32G4, selected data is packetized in firmware, and the EG25 sends it to the remote driver station over LTE.

The power tree converts unregulated vehicle power through a protected input stage, buck regulation, and local filtering to support the microcontroller, LTE modem, SIM interface, CAN transceiver, and digital circuitry. Revision 2 is the vehicle-ready version: cleaner layout, more stable power architecture, better input protection, and a four-layer stackup for cleaner signal routing.

> Main design constraint: add cellular telemetry without disrupting the racecar's existing CAN network.

## Interfaces and implementation

The board centers on an STM32G4 embedded controller that receives vehicle CAN data and communicates with the Quectel EG25 LTE modem over UART. The SIM section supports cellular connectivity, while the power section handles the backplane input, eFuse protection, buck regulation, LDO regulation, ferrite beads, and local filtering. R2 moves the design onto a four-layer PCB, giving the CAN and UART routes cleaner reference planes and improving signal integrity compared with the first revision.

## PCB revisions: R1 to R2

R1 proved the basic system concept and helped me understand the mechanical constraints, connector placement, STM32 routing, LTE interface, SIM section, and CAN path. R2 is where the design became much more intentional. The layout is cleaner because I had more Altium experience, but the bigger change is that the electrical architecture became more defensible.

## Firmware and data path

I developed the embedded firmware in C using STM32CubeIDE and STM32 HAL libraries. The firmware acquires CAN frames, extracts the required vehicle data, builds 8-byte telemetry payloads, and transfers them to the LTE modem.

The system reached approximately **3,200 telemetry packets per second** with approximately **2-3 milliseconds of end-to-end latency**, providing live visibility into vehicle metrics during operation.

## Bring-up and validation

I used oscilloscopes and digital multimeters to verify power rails, signal behavior, EMI performance, and subsystem integration. Validation progressed from individual power and communication blocks to full CAN-to-LTE operation before deployment on the racecar.

Next additions: scope captures, final assembled board photos, and synchronized vehicle-side versus Grafana data. R1 proved the board concept. R2 updated the power architecture, protection, stackup, and signal routing for vehicle use.
